# Blueberry harvesting machine: types, costs and what actually works on fresh-market farms

A blueberry harvesting machine is not one product: it is three different tools for three different jobs. Over-the-row harvesters strip a bush in minutes but bruise fruit and take green berries with ripe ones. Walk-behind shakers suit processing fruit. Selective machines pick berry by berry and are the only category that keeps fresh-market grade. The choice depends on where your berries go after the field, not on how fast the machine drives.

Below is what each type actually does, what it costs per kilogram of picked fruit, and what to check before signing for one.

## The three types of blueberry harvesting machines

**Over-the-row harvesters.** A tractor-mounted or self-propelled frame drives over the row and shakes the bush with beaters. Throughput is high, labour per hectare drops sharply, but the machine cannot tell a green berry from a ripe one. Fruit goes to freezing or processing, where a share of bruising and a few percent of loss are acceptable. For fresh-market packing, this is where most of the loss appears: about 5% of the crop stays in the field or leaves the premium grade.

**Walk-behind and small-tractor shakers.** Cheaper to buy and to run on small plots. Same logic as the big harvesters: whole-bush shaking, mixed ripeness, processing-grade fruit. On pick-your-own or farm-gate operations they can still make sense, but not for a punnet that has to travel to a supermarket.

**Selective harvesting robots.** A new and still small category. Instead of shaking the bush, a robot looks at each berry, decides whether it is ripe, and picks it with a soft gripper. The point is not raw speed — it is that the fruit never gets mixed, never gets bruised, and never waits for a picking crew to arrive.

## What fresh-market blueberries require

Supermarket and export programmes pay for consistency: berry size within a narrow calibre band, no soft fruit, no bruising, and a cool chain that starts within hours of picking. Three machine characteristics decide whether you meet that:

1. **Selectivity.** Ripe berries only, per berry, not per bush.
2. **Grip force.** Berries bruise at surprisingly low loads; anything above roughly 2 N of contact force on a single fruit starts to show as soft spots two days later in the punnet.
3. **Time from bush to cooler.** A machine that works 12-14 hours a day without a crew rotation compresses the harvest window instead of stretching it.

## What a blueberry harvesting machine costs per kilogram

Purchase price is the number everyone asks about first, and it is the number that tells you the least.

- **Manual picking** costs roughly €2.4 per kilogram in Western Europe in 2026, and that figure moves up every season because seasonal labour is scarcer each year.
- **A purchased machine** puts the whole cost into the year you buy it. For over-the-row harvesters that means a six-figure investment tied to one crop and one season window — the machine sits idle for ten months.
- **Robot-as-a-Service** moves the cost to the season: service, software, updates and support included, paid per season rather than as capital expenditure.

## Five questions to ask before buying any harvesting machine

1. **What is the measured crop loss?** Ask for the number from a real field, in kilograms per hectare, not a percentage from a brochure.
2. **What is the bruise rate after 48 hours?** Fruit that looks fine on the trailer can be soft in the punnet two days later. Test the machine with your own variety.
3. **What throughput does it hold all day?** Peak throughput on a demo plot and sustained throughput over a 12-hour shift are different numbers.
4. **Who services it, and how fast?** A harvest window does not wait for a spare part. Ask for the service-level agreement in hours, not days.
5. **What data do you get back?** Ripeness distribution, yield per row and cost per kilogram are the numbers that make next season cheaper.

## FAQ

**Does a harvesting machine work for fresh-market blueberries?**
Only selective machines. Over-the-row harvesters and shakers mix ripeness and bruise fruit, which is acceptable for processing but not for supermarket or export programmes.

**What does a blueberry harvesting machine cost?**
It depends on the model of payment. Manual picking costs about €2.4/kg. Robot-as-a-Service for CALIBER+ is €26,000 per season (about €1.9/kg), and purchase starts at €55,000 for the four-arm Lite configuration and €80,000 for the six-arm Flagship.

**Can a robot replace hand picking completely?**
Not yet. A selective robot covers a full working day and a large share of a crew's output, but on most farms the first seasons are a hybrid: the robot picks the bulk, people handle the rest. The gain is that the harvest no longer depends on whether the crew arrives.
